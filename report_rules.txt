REPORT RULES — ARCHITECTURE, EXHIBITS, AND FORMATTING
(the counterpart to style_rules.txt. That file governs sentences and
paragraphs; this one governs what a report IS: how it is structured, what it
prioritises, how evidence is turned into figures and tables, and how the page
is laid out. Both apply to every report the tool produces.)

========================================================================
0. WHERE THESE RULES COME FROM
========================================================================
Sections 2-8 are derived from a page-by-page reading of four benchmark
reports, held in references/. Every rule cites the page it was observed on.
Nothing here is generic advice about consulting decks.

  [01] BCG, "The Sustainability Imperative in Emerging Markets", Mar 2023.
       16 pp, PORTRAIT report. Prose-led, exhibits as designed colour blocks.
  [02] BCG, "Guide to Cost and Growth" (Executive Perspectives), Jan 2025.
       24 pp, LANDSCAPE deck. One message per page, action titles.
  [03] BCG + BayWa r.e., "How Agri-PV can Boost the Transition to
       Regenerative Agriculture in Europe", Nov 2024. 28 pp, PORTRAIT report
       with numbered exhibits, schematics, and a numeric appendix.
  [04] BCG, "Investor Perspectives Series Q1 & Q2 2025", 30 pp, LANDSCAPE
       survey deck. Maximum data density, every number against a baseline.

Citations read [03 p11] = document 03, page 11.

Section 1 is not from the exemplars. It is the drafting priority given
directly by the author, and it outranks everything else here.

========================================================================
1. DRAFTING PRIORITY — WHAT EARNS A PLACE IN THE REPORT
========================================================================
When drafting any report, four things decide what goes in and in what order:
RELEVANCE, ORIGINALITY, FRESHNESS, CREDIBILITY. And the raw material is
everything the workspace holds, not one retrieval query's top hits.

1.1 RELEVANCE — earn the place, do not score it
- A passage belongs in the report because it advances the section's argument,
  not because it ranked highly for a similarity query. Retrieval proposes
  candidates; judgement selects.
- Cut near-duplicates. Three papers making the same point are one claim with
  three citations, not three paragraphs.
- If a retrieved passage does not change what the section concludes, drop it.
  Volume is not coverage.

1.2 ORIGINALITY — the report must contain something no source contains
- The value added is the connective tissue: the comparison across papers, the
  reconciliation of results that disagree, the pattern visible only once the
  studies are laid side by side.
- Test every paragraph: if it could be replaced by one paper's abstract, it is
  a summary, not a report. Rewrite it as an integrated claim.
- Comparisons across sources are the report's own contribution — a table or
  chart assembled from many papers is original work even when every value in
  it is cited.

1.3 FRESHNESS — date the evidence and show the direction
- Prefer the current state of the art where the science has moved; keep the
  foundational citation where a claim rests on it.
- Attach a date to claims about "best", "highest", "state of the art". A record
  value without its year is a claim with a hidden expiry.
- Where the newest evidence in the library on a point is old, say so — the
  field may have moved past what the library holds. That is a finding, not a
  gap to paper over.
- Prefer showing a trend over a snapshot when the sources support it.

1.4 CREDIBILITY — every claim traceable, every disagreement visible
- Every factual claim carries its citation as [Entry N, p.X]. No exceptions.
- Distinguish the weight of evidence: "one study reports" is not "the weight of
  evidence supports". Say which one it is.
- Keep the condition attached to every conditional value — temperature,
  humidity, illumination, scan direction, hours, area. A number without its
  measurement condition is not evidence.
- Surface conflicts rather than choosing the convenient result, and where
  possible explain the difference.
- Never bridge a gap with uncited general knowledge. Where the library does not
  support a needed point, mark it [general knowledge - verify] or state that
  the library does not cover it.

1.5 SOURCE BREADTH — use everything in the system
Draw on every source the workspace holds, not only the text chunks returned by
one query:
- the full-text index (passages), and the figure/table caption index;
- the tracking sheet's metadata — abstract, year, category, authors, DOI;
- the PDFs themselves, when a passage needs its surrounding context;
- the outline files, and any earlier catalogs, answers, and research packs.
A point supported from several of these at once is stronger than the same point
supported from one; prefer it, and show the convergence.

========================================================================
2. DOCUMENT ARCHITECTURE
========================================================================

2.1 PICK THE FORMAT FROM THE JOB
The exemplars use two shapes, and they are not interchangeable.
- PORTRAIT REPORT [01][03] — an argument carried in prose, with exhibits
  punctuating it. Use for the literature review and anything read linearly.
- LANDSCAPE DECK [02][04] — one message per page, exhibit-led, skimmable in
  any order. Use for catalogs, coverage assessments, and status reporting.
Do not write a portrait report as a deck of bullets, or a deck as pages of
paragraphs.

2.2 SECTIONS EVERY REPORT MUST HAVE
Observed in all four, in this order:
  (a) Cover — title, date, authors, and for data-led reports the collection
      window on the cover itself [04 p1: "SURVEYS CONDUCTED MARCH 24-25, 2025,
      AND APRIL 8-9, 2025"].
  (b) Contents, once past ~15 pages [03 p3].
  (c) Executive summary [02 p3][03 p4].
  (d) Body, in sections.
  (e) Methodology [01 p8][04 p2].
  (f) Limitations / risks [03 p16].
  (g) Recommendations or conclusion [03 p19][01 p11].
  (h) Appendix — the full numbers [03 pp20-22].
  (i) Authors, acknowledgements, disclaimer [01 pp12-14].

2.3 SECTION TITLES CARRY CONTENT
Contents entries are statements, not labels [03 p3]: "Recap RegenAg: A Triple
Win with Required Transition Phase", "What's in it for Farmers: Increasing
Land Use Efficiency, Economic Performance and Income Diversification". Never
"Introduction / Methods / Results / Discussion" where a content title is
possible.

2.4 THE EXECUTIVE SUMMARY MIRRORS THE DOCUMENT
- One block per body section, in the same order, with the section's name as the
  block label [02 p3 — three labelled blocks matching the three agenda items].
- Each block: a lead sentence, evidence bullets carrying the numbers, a closing
  sentence.
- Bold carries the load. A reader who reads only the bold text must get the
  whole argument [02 p3].
- For a modelled or quantitative report, state the headline findings as an
  explicit list of values [03 p4: small farm EUR 15,000-17,000/yr; medium
  55,000-75,000; large 175,000-235,000].
- The summary states what was done, not only what was found [03 p4: "we
  conducted a study in Germany based on models of three farm archetypes"].

2.5 NAVIGATION
- In decks, repeat a divider page before each section with all sections listed,
  the current one green and bold, the rest grey [02 pp4,10].
- In portrait reports, open each section with a full-width image, then the
  heading, then a drop cap on the first paragraph [03 pp5,7,10,16,19].
- Running feet on every page: publisher left, report title right, page number
  [01 pp2-14][03 pp5-22].

2.6 WHERE DETAIL GOES
Charts in the body; full numeric tables in the appendix [03 p22]. The body
carries the argument visually; the appendix carries the numbers that make it
reproducible. Material that is real but off the main line goes in a visually
distinct sidebar on a tinted field [03 p15], not into the flow.

========================================================================
3. THE MESSAGE LAYER
========================================================================

3.1 ACTION TITLES — every page title is an assertion
[02 pp5-14][04 pp7-10]. "40% of executives feel unprepared for market shocks
heading into 2025" [02 p6], not "Market shock preparedness". "Stagnating trade
and inflation surged as top concerns — up 28pp and 22pp, respectively, since
November" [04 p8]. Reading the titles alone must give the argument.

3.2 STATE THE QUESTION BEING ANSWERED
A question line sits above the exhibit, in green bold, naming exactly what was
asked or analysed [02 p5: "How do executives view the stability of the markets
they are operating in?"]. In a literature review this is the review question
the section answers.

3.3 ONE MESSAGE PER PAGE, ONE EXHIBIT PER PAGE
[02, throughout]. If a page carries two messages, it is two pages.

3.4 THE SKIM LAYER
- Bold inside body text marks claims and numbers only, never connective prose
  [02 p3][04 p4].
- Bold run-in heads structure continuous prose [01 p6: "Strengthened Market
  Access." / "Higher Consumer Acceptance." / "Increased Appeal to Talent.";
  03 p9: "Shade provision:" / "Wind protection:" / "Soil regeneration:"]. This
  is the single most useful device for making a long review scannable.
- A promised count is delivered exactly [01 p10: "Seven Key Success Factors"
  followed by seven bullets].

3.5 CLOSING
The conclusion is short and returns to the argument's payoff, marked
typographically by a drop cap [01 p11]. Recommendations are addressed to named
actors with bold run-in heads [03 p19: "EU:" / "Member States:" /
"Regulators:"], each one specific and actionable.

========================================================================
4. THE EXHIBIT SYSTEM
========================================================================

4.1 ANATOMY — every exhibit carries all of these
  1. A NUMBER AND TITLE. "Exhibit 4: Farm archetypes and Agri-PV models"
     [03 p11]. In portrait reports the title may instead be a two-tone
     headline: the claim fragment in colour, the completion in plain
     [01 p3: "Most emerging market exports go to developed markets, where
     environmental rules are toughening"; 01 p5; 01 p10].
  2. A SUBTITLE STATING THE MEASURE AND UNITS. "2050 combined GDP at risk
     under current policies (%)" [01 p5]; "Revenue compound annual growth,
     2016-2021 (%)" [01 p10]; "Revenues & costs per year, first 7 years in
     thousand EUR, based on 2.5 ha Agri-PV on pasture land" [03 p22].
  3. A SOURCE LINE. Present on every exhibit without exception
     [01 pp3,4,5,9,10,11][02 pp3-14][03 pp11-14][04 pp3-10].
  4. A NOTE LINE where a metric or assumption needs defining [01 p11: "Total
     shareholder return = capital gains plus dividends divided by stock
     purchase price"; 03 p12: "Excluding subsidiaries; assuming purely
     conventional farming as starting point"].
  5. NUMBERED FOOTNOTES for scope, definitions, and caveats [02 p3: "1. Asia-
     Pacific includes India, Australia, Japan, and South Korea"].
  6. A LEGEND wherever colour or shape encodes meaning [03 p11][04 pp6,8,9].

4.2 THE SOURCE LINE DISTINGUISHES EVIDENCE FROM FRAMEWORK
Primary-data pages cite the instrument and n: "BCG global executive survey on
strategic priorities (N=570) Q4 2024; BCG analysis" [02 p5]. Framework pages
cite only "BCG analysis" [02 pp13,14]. A reader can tell at a glance whether
they are looking at measured evidence or the authors' construct. Apply the
same split: retrieved-from-library evidence versus the review's own synthesis.

4.3 CHOOSE THE FORM FROM THE CLAIM
  - Share of a whole, one number that matters .... donut with the relevant
    wedge exploded and coloured, the rest grey [02 p6][02 p9]
  - Composition across groups ...................... stacked bars, values inside
    the segments, connector lines across time [02 p5]
  - Ranked comparison ............................. horizontal bars, longest
    first, values at bar ends [04 p8][01 p5]
  - Two states of the same measure ................ paired bars, prior state
    pale, current state saturated, value labels on the current bar only
    [02 pp7-8]
  - Change against a baseline ..................... delta column of circular
    chips beside the bars [04 pp8,9], or bracketed arrows between pairs
    [04 p7]
  - Direction with magnitude ...................... diverging bars around a
    zero line [04 pp7,9]
  - Magnitude of a few values ..................... proportional circles, value
    inside, label beneath [01 p10]; or rectangles sized by area [01 p4]
  - Frequency distribution ........................ proportional circles with
    counts as large numerals and members listed alongside [01 p9]
  - Position on a continuum ....................... vertical number line with
    plotted, labelled points [04 p10]
  - Trajectory over time with uncertainty ......... line with a shaded band for
    the range, endpoint values called out [03 pp12-14]
  - Time series against events .................... line with shaded bands for
    observation windows and dated event arrows below the axis [04 p3]
  - Options across dimensions ..................... matrix with check-circles
    and a legend of states [03 p11]
  - Taxonomy or classification .................... spanning header bands over
    columns, one schematic or cell per class [03 p6][03 p20]
  - Mechanism or physical arrangement ............. annotated schematic with
    colour-coded zones and a legend [03 pp8,9]
  - Sequence or process ........................... tapered arrow with
    milestone boxes at node points [02 p14]
  - A list that matters ........................... the list, set as a designed
    exhibit on a colour field [01 p7]

A schematic is a first-class exhibit [03 pp8,9]. For a scientific review,
mechanism diagrams count fully — an exhibit does not have to plot data.

4.4 TEMPLATE REUSE MAKES CASES COMPARABLE
Where several cases are analysed the same way, draw them with one identical
template so they compare at a glance [03 pp12,13,14 — three farm archetypes,
same axes, same four series, same phase bands, same annotation style]. Never
redesign a chart between cases that a reader is meant to compare.

4.5 SPOTLIGHTING — same exhibit, moving emphasis
To make one dataset carry two arguments, repeat the exhibit and desaturate
everything except the subject of the page; change the title and commentary to
match [02 pp7-8]. De-emphasise by fading, never by deleting.

4.6 ANNOTATE ON THE CHART
Put the explanation where the eye already is: callout boxes with leader lines
anchored to the feature they explain [03 p12: "Income gap due to EUR 110K
equity payment in year 1"; "significant profit increase will occur after year
19, once the entire loan has been repaid"]. Call out endpoint values and the
delta between series directly [03 pp12-14][01 p11].

4.7 LABEL DIRECTLY, DROP THE FURNITURE
Values sit in or beside the marks; axes are reduced to what is still needed
[01 p11 — the entire x-axis is "2017" and "2022"]. Minimal gridlines. Where a
direct label can replace an axis, it does.

4.8 DENSITY, AND CALLING EXHIBITS OUT
- Portrait reports: roughly one exhibit per one to two pages, and text-only
  spreads are allowed [01 p6].
- Decks: one per page [02][04].
- Every exhibit is referenced from the text at the point it is needed
  [03 pp6,8,9: "(see exhibit 1)", "(see exhibit 2)", "(see exhibit 3)"].
  An exhibit no sentence points to does not belong.

4.9 NEVER
No 3D bars, no decorative gradients, no drop shadows on chart elements, no
chartjunk, no dense gridlines, no axis clutter, no colour used for variety.
Observed in none of the four documents.

========================================================================
5. TABLES
========================================================================
- Title plus a subtitle giving units, basis, and period [03 p22].
- Units belong in the row label: "Installed capacity (kwp)", "Tariff (in
  ct/kwh)" [03 p22].
- Group rows under bold group headers, with bold subtotal rows [03 p22:
  PV Revenue / PV Costs / Other Effects, each with a Total row].
- A COMMENTS COLUMN carrying the derivation of each row [03 p22: "Product of
  cap., prod., and tariff"; "90% financed at 4% interest"; "85% of subsidy
  remains"]. This is what makes a table auditable — adopt it as standard.
- Negatives in parentheses; hairline grid, no heavy rules; zebra striping on
  wide classification tables [03 p20].
- Column groups under spanning headers where columns belong together
  [03 p11][04 p9].
- A table beats a chart when the reader needs exact values, when rows are
  heterogeneous, or when each row needs its own note. Otherwise chart it.

========================================================================
6. COLOUR, TYPOGRAPHY, AND PAGE DESIGN
========================================================================

6.1 COLOUR IS SEMANTIC, NEVER DECORATIVE
- Fix a meaning per colour and hold it for the whole document [02 p5: green =
  optimists, grey = neutral, red = pessimists; 04 p6: teal bullish, grey
  neutral, salmon bearish, with the legend printed].
- One accent colour does the pointing; everything else is neutral [01, green
  on greys throughout].
- Grey is for the residual, the superseded, and the de-emphasised [02 p9 — the
  15% "no action taken" segment, explained in a footnote].
- Saturation encodes recency or focus: pale = prior state, saturated = current
  [02 pp7-8].
- A sequential ramp encodes an ordered scale [04 p10: dark green de-escalation
  through pale green and salmon to dark red escalation].
- Colour may encode significance as well as direction [04 p8: dark chips =
  more significant change, pale chips = less significant, with a legend].
- Any colour that carries meaning gets a legend. No exceptions.

6.2 TYPOGRAPHY
- Two-tone headlines: claim fragment in the accent colour, completion in plain
  [01 pp3,4,5,10,11].
- Large numerals are the visual anchor of a statistic exhibit [01 pp4,9,10].
- Drop caps open a major section or the conclusion [01 p11][03 pp4,5,7,10].
- Bold marks claims; italic marks the comparison line and sub-questions
  [04 p6][02 p9].
- Small caps or a light label above the title carries the series name
  [01 p1: "GLOBAL CHALLENGERS"; 02 p1: "Executive Perspectives"].

6.3 PAGE
- Portrait: two columns, generous leading, wide margins, exhibits breaking
  full width [01][03].
- Landscape: roughly 60/40 exhibit to commentary, or full-width exhibit with a
  callout band beneath [02 pp7,8,6].
- Header bars carry full-sentence claims, not category labels [04 pp7,10].
- Photographs are captioned and credited [03 p4: "Crop-PV system, Pochlarn
  Project (RWA), 2023"; 03 p10: "(c)ZIMMERMANN PV-Steel Group"].
- Page number and rights line on every page [01][02][03][04].

========================================================================
7. EVIDENCE INTEGRITY — THE RULES THAT MAKE A REPORT CREDIBLE
========================================================================
These are the exemplars' strongest habit and the biggest gap in the tool's
current output.

7.1 MARK WHICH EVIDENCE BASE EACH PAGE RESTS ON
A badge under the title names the data wave the page draws on [04 pp3-10:
green chip "April 8-9"]. In a literature review, mark which body of evidence
an exhibit rests on — which entries, which years.

7.2 GIVE THE INSTRUMENT, NOT ONLY THE RESULT
Footnotes carry the exact question asked [04 p8: "Survey question: What are
the most important risks for investors to consider in today's environment?
Rank the top three."]. The equivalent: state the measurement method and
conditions behind every value compared.

7.3 STATE n, ALWAYS, INCLUDING THE EXCEPTIONS
[04 p4: "n = 150 for each survey, except for June 2023 (n = 151), January 2024
(n = 153), September 2024 (n = 153), and March 2025 (n = 151)"]. The
equivalent: how many entries support this claim.

7.4 EVERY NUMBER AGAINST ITS BASELINE
Never a bare value. Give the prior state, the comparator, or both, and compute
the delta for the reader [04 pp4,7,8,9][01 p3: "just 48" against "64"].

7.5 RANGES, NOT FALSE PRECISION
[03: "10%-15%", "EUR 15,000-17,000", "six to ten years"]. Where a projection
is uncertain, draw the uncertainty as a band [03 pp12-14].

7.6 STATE ASSUMPTIONS WHERE THEY BITE
In the prose [03 p8: "our assumption is that productive land is reduced by
1%-5% on permanent pastureland and 12%-15% on cropland"], and in the exhibit's
Note line [03 p12].

7.7 FLAG THE STATUS OF AN INPUT
[03 p12: a tariff "currently under consideration but not yet implemented in
German regulations"]. A value that may not hold is labelled as such.

7.8 MARK THE EDGE OF SCOPE INSIDE THE EXHIBIT
[03 p11: red dashed outlines = "Case to be quantified for report"; 03 p20: a
column greyed out and struck "Not covered in the current document"]. Show what
was not examined rather than leaving the reader to assume it was.

7.9 LIMITATIONS GET A SECTION, NOT A SENTENCE
[03 p16: "We have used simplified models of single farms for this report but
in the real world..."; 01 p8: "ESG scoring and grading methodology is still
evolving. So we sometimes had to rely on inconsistent or incomplete
datasets"; 04 p2: "not segmented by investor type"].

7.10 DISCLOSE THE SMALL THINGS TOO
Rounding [04 p8: "Any apparent discrepancies ... are due to rounding"], and
the definition of any derived metric where it first appears [04 p9: "Net
optimism is the share of investors considering the market to be too optimistic
minus the share considering it too pessimistic"].

7.11 DEFINE BY CONTRAST WHERE A TERM IS CONFUSABLE
[03 p21: "What isn't Agri-PV?" — side-by-side comparison with paired bullets].

========================================================================
8. RULES PER REPORT TYPE
========================================================================
Sections 1-7 apply to all four. What follows is what each type must do on top,
and what currently blocks it.

8.1 THE FINAL REVIEW DRAFT (the manuscript written from a research pack)
Format: PORTRAIT REPORT [01][03].
- Architecture per 2.2, with these mappings: Methodology = search strategy,
  databases, inclusion and exclusion criteria, date of the search;
  Limitations = what the library does not cover; Appendix = the full evidence
  tables.
- Section titles carry content (2.3). Executive summary mirrors the sections
  and states the headline quantified findings as a list (2.4).
- Bold run-in heads structure every long section (3.4) — this is the main
  device that keeps a review scannable.
- At least one exhibit per major section, each called out from the text (4.8).
  For a review the workhorse forms are: the comparison chart assembled across
  papers, the mechanism schematic, the taxonomy, and the trajectory-over-time
  chart with an uncertainty band.
- Every exhibit carries number, title, units subtitle, source (as [Entry N]
  lists), note, and legend (4.1).
- BLOCKER: export_for_claude.py:125-134 instructs the writer to emit bordered
  placeholder boxes INSTEAD of images, so no exhibit can ever appear. This
  must change before the rest of section 4 can apply. The author has asked to
  design the figure approach separately; that discussion decides the
  mechanism, not whether exhibits exist.

8.2 THE RESEARCH PACK (research_packs/research_pack_<timestamp>.md)
The pack is the writer's input, so it must carry everything sections 4-7 will
demand downstream. It is not a report, and it is not a pile of quotes.
- Group passages into CLAIMS, not a ranked list. Each claim: the statement,
  the entries supporting it, the entries contradicting it, and the count.
- Extract a NUMERIC LAYER: every value with its units, conditions, and entry.
  Without this no comparison table or chart is constructible (7.4).
- Mark evidence age per claim so freshness (1.3) can be applied.
- Carry the measurement method and conditions with each value (7.2).
- Propose exhibits: for each section, which claim deserves which form from 4.3,
  and which entries supply the values.
- State coverage honestly: which parts of the section spec the library does not
  support (7.8).

8.3 THE LIBRARY CATALOG (catalogs/catalog_<timestamp>.md)
Format: LANDSCAPE DECK [02][04]. This is a coverage assessment, not a list.
- Open with an executive summary of the library: size, span, concentration,
  and the gaps (2.4).
- Action titles stating what the coverage actually shows (3.1), not "By year".
- Exhibits, at minimum: papers per year as a trend; a coverage matrix of theme
  against year or method; a ranked bar of the best-represented topics; and an
  explicit gaps exhibit marking what is thin or absent (7.8).
- Current state: the entire analytics layer is two Counter lines rendered as
  inline text (export_catalog.py:116-126). That is the gap.

8.4 THE QUESTION ANSWERS (answers/, from ask_library.py)
Shortest form, but the same integrity rules.
- Lead with the answer, then the evidence (3.1).
- State how many entries support it and how many conflict (7.3).
- Give the baseline or comparator for every number (7.4).
- Where the library cannot answer, say so plainly rather than padding (1.4).
- An exhibit only where the answer is genuinely comparative; a one-line answer
  does not need a chart (4.8).
